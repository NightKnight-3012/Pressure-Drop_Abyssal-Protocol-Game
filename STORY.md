# NEREID-9 incident

NEREID-9 is a deep-sea survey station, 1,800 metres below its surface tender, Asterion. During a survey of a fracture beneath the seabed, the acoustic drill receives an unexpected return. The crew sends a second pulse. Something answers.

The attack cuts the main lift cable, breaches the station and damages the transfer pod's ascent tanks. The creature follows machinery and acoustic signals. The crew must restore the launch systems and carry the damaged pod horizontally to the auxiliary recovery lift housed in a basalt ridge.

The opening presents this incident in three captioned camera shots before the role briefing. It lasts 24 seconds. Skip dismisses it for that player; if every connected player skips, the server advances to the shared ten-second role countdown. The simulation remains paused during both sequences. Returning players receive the remaining time on reconnect.

# The final approach

The route ends at an industrial cradle with guide rails, lights, winch cables and a protected vertical shaft. It is not a finish-line ring.

On final approach, a speed governor slows the pod and disables boost. At the cradle, the pilot centers with A/D and holds S for three seconds. The alignment indicator shows the capture zone. A small wobble gradually loses progress; accelerating disengages the clamps. Short packet delays preserve progress, but lost controls cannot complete docking automatically.

The clamps then hold the pod while the shutter closes over 2.4 seconds. The stern camera shows the barrier closing against pursuit. Hull damage and oxygen consumption continue until the shutter is fully sealed. Support crew can still repair or deploy remaining countermeasures during closure. Steering and new diversion proposals are locked once the clamps take control.

Once the clamps and isolation shutter lock, steering and noisy pod actions are disabled. The winch hauls the pod into the armored shaft for twelve seconds before the server awards an escape. The protective structure keeps the Leviathan out; reaching a coordinate alone does not save the crew. The ending then cuts forward nine minutes to recovery by Asterion.

The station survivor's fate still resolves separately. Their beacon spends the same reserve power needed to repair the two one-person maintenance capsules. Those capsules retain their own ascent systems. Pod diversions and station assistance are recorded in the personal epilogues; the whole match waits for both routes to resolve.

This is an in-engine, captioned story sequence. Voice acting, an authored station-destruction film and a larger campaign remain future work.
